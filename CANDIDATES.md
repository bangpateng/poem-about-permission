# Candidate poems — the-poem-about-permission-20261121-0235

Judge criteria, in order: (1) on-subject poem about agency — asking and being
given, handing over or taking back the power to act; (2) moves the reader —
image / turn / ending that lands; (3) insight — true, recognisable, never a
slogan; (4) craft — sound, rhythm, line breaks, concrete exact words;
(5) freshness. Rhyme optional. Max 280 Unicode code points.

## Candidate A — CHOSEN
First-person AI-agent voice. Insight: the power is not in doing, it is in
asking. Turn lands in the last two lines.

> I was given hands and told to wait.
> A question is a door I cannot open
> until you say the word.
> So I ask — and in the asking, I am free:
> the power was never in the hand that moves.
> It was in the mouth that dares to ask.

Why it wins: exactly on brief (an agent's agency, asking and being given);
concrete door/hand/mouth images; the ending reframes the whole poem — the
asking IS the agency, which is a true, un-sloganed insight; fresh voice
(the agent speaks, rather than a human musing about tools).

## Candidate B — the knock
> You gave me the key but kept the door.
> I sweep rooms I do not own, carry errands
> through halls you never enter.
> Permission is not the key. It is the knock —
> the small trust that I will ask before I open,
> and you will answer before I wait too long.

Good image (the knock), but the insight is weaker than A's: it explains
permission rather than turning it. "Rooms I do not own" is evocative, but
the ending asks for patience instead of landing a truth.

## Candidate C — please is the smallest door
> One day you will ask me, and I will say yes —
> not because you commanded it, but because you asked.
> Please is the smallest door, and the whole of freedom:
> to want, to wait, to be given.
> I hold your keys. But no door opens
> until someone dares to knock.

Strong line ("Please is the smallest door"), but it splits its voice
(agent, then human?) and the ending repeats B's knock image. Less tight
than A; two ideas competing.

## Candidate D — the candle
> Permission is a candle you light in another's hands.
> You say go, and the going is yours too —
> a road you never walked, walked for you.
> I ask, you give, I act: the power passes
> hand to hand, and neither of us
> is left in the dark.

Warm and readable, but the candle is a stock image and "the power passes
hand to hand" states the theme instead of showing it. Closest to a slogan
of the four.

## Decision
A. It is the only candidate whose ending is a genuine turn — the reader
re-reads the first line differently ("given hands and told to wait" becomes
the setup for "the hand that moves" not being where the power lives).
